This introduction helps you compare unmet local needs, customer groups, and feasible business shapes **before** you lock a launch date or spend buildout capital. ## Core content

### Why “business shape” comes before branding

Pharmacy businesses are constrained by **what the license authorizes**, not only by marketing language. In Texas, Class A authorizes community dispensing to the public under a prescription drug order and may not engage in sterile compounding (use A-S for sterile) [1]. Statute lists classifications including Class A community, Class C institutional/ASC, Class D clinic, and Class E nonresident [2]. Each principal place of business needs a separate license; only one license may be issued for a specific location [3]. Choosing the wrong class wastes application fees — and a mid-review change of ownership, managing officer, or location cancels the Texas application [4].

Federally, any pharmacy that dispenses controlled substances must be DEA-registered; a state license is required; new pharmacies use Form 224; each place of business is separately registered [7][8]. NPI enrollment is a separate CMS path (NPPES web, EFI, or CMS-10114) [13]. Qualifying traditional pharmacies may be exempt from FDA establishment registration/listing under 21 CFR 207.13 — but that exemption does not relieve other obligations, and manufacturing beyond regular retail course can lose it [11]. Compounding models split between 503A (state-licensed pharmacy / physician, patient-specific framework) and 503B (outsourcing facility registration/reporting) [10].

### Market context you may use — carefully

Peer-reviewed work shows US community pharmacy annual turnover around 6.2% (2010–2023), with independent/franchise cumulative turnover substantially higher than chain [9]. More pharmacies closed than opened in 2018–21; independents and pharmacies in Black and Latinx communities were most at risk [19]. Rural pharmacy access relies disproportionately on independent/franchise locations in GIS analysis [18]. Qualitative work links payment models (including Part D DIR evolution) to sustainability concerns [20]. **Use these as diligence context**, not as a promise that your ZIP code will support a new store — and never as invented ROI [held: 01-C016].

## Setting branches

### Community (independent / retail)
Open-door or closed-door community dispensing is the default Class A path in Texas [1][2]. Screen hours, counseling space, delivery footprint, and whether sterile compounding would force Class A-S [1]. Rural access often depends on independent and franchise pharmacies [9] — treat that as need context, not a guaranteed demand signal. Independent pharmacies experience higher ownership churn than chains [8]; build cash and coverage contingencies before a launch date.

### ASC / consulting pharmacy
ASC medication rooms and institutional pharmacies follow Class C / C-S paths and typically require HHSC facility licensure sequencing before TSBP can issue [12]. Do not treat a consulting agreement as a pharmacy license. Map who is accountable for procurement, storage, and controlled-substance records between facility leadership and the consultant pharmacist.

### Clinic-embedded
Texas Class D is a limited-formulary clinic pharmacy; TSBP notes a Class D license shall not be issued to a physician's office [1][14]. Confirm whether the site needs Class D versus full Class A. Separate clinic entity cash from pharmacy entity cash in your worksheets.

### Specialty / infusion / compounding
Decide early whether the model is patient-specific 503A compounding in a state-licensed pharmacy or a 503B outsourcing facility with FDA registration and reporting [4][10]. Texas sterile work requires the sterile subclass (A-S / C-S) — Class A alone may not engage in sterile compounding [1]. USP chapter operational details are **held** pending primary fetch (see Unresolved).

### Telepharmacy / delivery
In-state remote services and Class E / E-S nonresident ship-to-Texas paths differ [1][2]. Multi-state DEA registration is location-specific [3][7]. Delivery-to-residence counseling and temperature-integrity rules are covered more fully in LIB03; for startup, flag delivery cost as a distinct cash line (see LIB05 for claim cash).
